Backup and Recovery Checklist

Practical steps to protect family photographs, documents, business records and website data.

Find important data

List irreplaceable files and where they are stored: phones, laptops, external drives, websites, email accounts and online services. Include configurations and databases that are necessary to rebuild a system.

Keep separate copies

The 3-2-1 rule is a useful starting point: three copies, two storage types, one copy in another location. File synchronisation is not necessarily a backup because accidental deletions and corruption can synchronise as well.

Automate the routine

Schedule backups and check their most recent successful completion. Use encryption for sensitive data, strong unique credentials and multi-factor authentication for cloud accounts.

Test restoration

Periodically restore a selection of files to a separate location. Open them and check that their contents are readable. A backup job reporting success does not prove that recovery will work.

Protect against ransomware

Where practical, maintain a disconnected, immutable or otherwise protected backup that malware and compromised accounts cannot easily delete. Do not rely on a single always-attached drive.

Document recovery

Write down where backups are located, who has authorised access, what tools are required and how keys can be recovered. Store this information securely and separately from the computer being backed up.

Revisit the plan

Update the backup process after buying a new device, reorganising folders, launching a new website or adding databases. Check retention periods and available free space.
