Ground Support Crew — Business Planning Notes

Compiled from planning conversation, updated 30 September 2026.

1. The Idea

A company providing "chief of staff" style support — scheduling, coordination, provider follow-up, and day-to-day organisational load — for people who need it.

Originally planned to be self-funded via the founder's own NDIS plan as the first customer, but after speaking with someone at the NDIS, this was found to be a significant conflict of interest and not worth pursuing that way.

Revised plan: three potential client directions being considered —

  • NDIS participants
  • Older people / aged care
  • Wealthy private clients wanting a personal "chief of staff"

To develop the service without income conflict, the founder will act as an unpaid "guinea pig" test case for the NDIS side — logging everything a future NDIS participant client would actually need — with no income from this test phase. His mother, who is elderly, will be the unpaid test case for the aged-care side.

Long-term vision: once proven, expand to real paying clients across these directions, and potentially branch into other support categories (cleaning, personal support work) and non-NDIS services (software development, websites) under the same company.

Growth approach: profits reinvested into the company rather than drawn as a director's salary.

3. Legal & Registration Plan (Australia, Pty Ltd)

Firm decision: register as a company (Pty Ltd), not operate as a sole trader.

Starting Monday, 5 October 2026 — company registration begins.

Steps, roughly in order:

  • Apply for Director ID via ABRS (abrs.gov.au) — free, one-time for life, tied to the person not any company. (Completed.)
  • Check company name availability on ASIC's register (Ground Support Crew Pty Ltd appears clear based on searches so far — not yet confirmed on the live ASIC register).
  • Register the company with ASIC (~$636).
  • Apply for ABN (free, near-instant once ACN exists).
  • Register a business name if different from the company name (~$47).
  • Open a business bank account.
  • Arrange insurance (public liability, professional indemnity at minimum; workers' comp once a worker is engaged).
  • Set up invoicing/accounting software (e.g. Xero).
  • Draft a service agreement template.
  • Use a registered office address that is not the director's home address, to keep the home address off the public ASIC record (arrangement still open).

Director status: the founder will be director and shareholder but not an employee, and will not draw a director's salary — no super obligation is triggered this way, and profits stay in the company. He will not personally deliver supports — only the engaged worker will.

A director's name and (if not otherwise arranged) registered office address are publicly searchable on ASIC's register; the Director ID number itself is never public.

6. Worker / Pay Structure

Two candidates discussed for the first worker: the founder's brother, or an existing NDIS support worker currently employed casually by a different provider (who has already agreed in principle).

Relevant NDIS support item: "Assistance with Personal Domestic Activities" — national price limit $61.16/hr (2026–27), used as the reference revenue rate for margin calculations.

Key employment-law point: even a worker engaged as an individual "contractor" (own ABN, no company) is very likely to be deemed an employee for superannuation purposes under the ATO's extended definition, since the arrangement is mainly about paying for their personal labour by the hour. So the contractor vs. casual-employee numbers end up very similar in practice. Genuine independence (paid per result, able to delegate, own tools/systems) would be needed to avoid the super obligation.

Decision leaning: casual employment with $35–40/hr as a starting rate, budgeting for super (12%) and workers' comp (~1–3%) on top. Not yet finalised.

Worker Screening portability: the NDIS Worker Screening Check is national, valid 5 years, and portable across employers/providers without reapplying — only a linking step is needed via the NDIS Worker Screening Database. If the chosen worker already holds an active clearance from their current job, this collapses from a 1–4 week wait to essentially a same-day admin task. A worker can hold clearance links to multiple employers/providers at once, and can work for more than one provider simultaneously — the only real constraint would be an exclusivity or non-compete clause in their existing employment contract, which is worth them checking.

7. NDIS Registration Requirement

Registration with the NDIS Quality & Safeguards Commission is NOT required for this type of support (registration is only mandatory for SIL and platform providers). Unregistered providers can serve plan-managed and self-managed participants.

A conflict-of-interest issue was raised directly with the NDIS: since the founder would have been both the business owner and the NDIS participant paying for the service, this was found to be a significant conflict that isn't worth pursuing — hence the pivot to the unpaid guinea-pig test approach and eventually seeking separate paying clients instead.

8. Overseas Travel (Bosnia trip) — NDIS Funding Notes

The trip itself (flights, accommodation, worker's travel costs) is never NDIS-fundable, regardless of company structure.

What may be fundable: the support worker's actual hours of support delivered during the trip (same kind of support as at home), capped at 6 weeks of continued funding while overseas, and only if it ties to an existing goal in the NDIS plan.

Recommended to confirm with the plan manager in writing before booking anything, to know exactly which hours would be accepted.
